📘 Chapter 1 — Foundations

Present Value
PV = FV / (1 + i)ⁿ
Value today of a future cash flow. i = interest rate, n = years
Perpetuity PV
PV = CF / i
Value of infinite constant cash flow CF at rate i
Accounting Profit
π_acc = TR − Explicit Costs
Revenue minus cash expenses only
Economic Profit
π_eco = TR − All Costs
Includes implicit/opportunity costs
Net Benefit Max
MB = MC at optimum Q
Maximize NB = TB − TC where MNB = 0
Marginal Net Benefit
MNB = MB − MC
Set MNB = 0 to find profit-maximizing Q

📗 Chapter 2 — Supply & Demand

Consumer Surplus
CS = ½ × base × height
Area above price below demand curve
Producer Surplus
PS = ½ × base × height
Area below price above supply curve
Excise Tax Effect
Supply shifts up by tax amount
Burden shared; P* rises less than full tax

📙 Chapter 3 — Elasticity

Own-Price Elasticity
E = %ΔQ / %ΔP
|E|>1 elastic, |E|<1 inelastic, |E|=1 unitary
Linear Demand E
E = b × (P/Q)
b = slope coefficient of demand equation
Log-Linear E
ln Q = a + b·ln P → E = b
Coefficient IS the elasticity directly
Marginal Revenue
MR = P × (1 + 1/E)
Links price, MR, and elasticity
Cross-Price Elasticity
E_xy = %ΔQx / %ΔPy
Positive = substitutes, Negative = complements
Income Elasticity
E_M = %ΔQ / %ΔM
Positive = normal, Negative = inferior, >1 = luxury

📒 Chapter 4 — Consumer Behavior

Budget Constraint
M = Px·X + Py·Y
Income equals spending on X and Y
Budget Line Slope
slope = −Px / Py
Rate at which X trades for Y in market
Consumer Equilibrium
MRS = Px / Py
Tangency of indifference curve and budget line
MRS
MRS = MUx / MUy
Rate consumer trades Y for X keeping utility constant

📕 Chapter 5 — Production & Cost

MRTS
MRTS = MPL / MPK
Rate at which L substitutes for K on isoquant
Isocost Line
C = wL + rK
Slope = −w/r; w=wage, r=rental rate of capital
Cost Minimization
MRTS = w/r → MPL/w = MPK/r
Isoquant tangent to isocost at optimum
VMPL
VMPL = P × MPL
Value of marginal product of labor
Profit Max — Labor
VMPL = w
Hire labor until value equals wage
Profit Max — Output
MR = MC
Produce until marginal revenue equals marginal cost

🏭 Chapter 6 — Market Structures

Competitive Output Rule
P = MC
Price-taking firm produces where price equals marginal cost
Shutdown Rule
Produce if P ≥ AVC; Shut down if P < AVC
In short run, cover variable costs to stay open
Long-Run Competitive Profit
π = 0 (P = min ATC)
Free entry/exit drives economic profit to zero
Monopoly Profit Max
MR = MC → find Q*, read P* from demand
Two-step: quantity from MR=MC, price from demand curve
Lerner Index
(P − MC) / P = −1/E
Measures monopoly power; higher = more power
Optimal Advertising
A/TR = −E_A / E
E_A = advertising elasticity, E = own-price elasticity

⚡ Chapter 9 — Oligopoly

Cournot Reaction Function
Q1 = f(Q2); Q2 = f(Q1)
Each firm's optimal output given rival's quantity; Nash equilibrium at intersection
Stackelberg Leader Output
Leader substitutes follower's reaction into own profit
First-mover advantage: leader produces more than Cournot
Bertrand Equilibrium
P = MC (competitive outcome)
Price competition with identical goods → zero profit paradox
Collusion PV
PV_coop = π_coop / r
Cooperate if PV(cooperation) > PV(cheating); lower r supports cooperation

🎲 Chapter 10 — Game Theory

Dominant Strategy
Best move regardless of rival's action
Check row by row (or column by column) — does one strategy always win?
Nash Equilibrium
No player gains by unilateral deviation
Best response to best response; stable but may not be efficient
Secure Strategy (Maximin)
max(min payoff)
Best worst-case outcome; used when no dominant strategy exists
Cooperation Condition
PV(cooperate) > PV(cheat)
π_coop/r > π_cheat; requires low interest rate, indefinite repetition
Subgame Perfect Equilibrium
Backward induction from last stage
Rules out non-credible threats; solve game tree from end to start
